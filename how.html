What this is and how it works

SD Flying Radar is a quick daily read of SoCal flying for the next 7 days. It reads the weather models the way an experienced pilot reads the usual weather tools and the NWS, and turns them into one score per site and day, so you see at a glance which day and which site deserve a closer look.

Optimistic by design: a score says how good the day can be from the numbers. It is tuned by hand against pilots' favourite weather tools and local pilots' reads, not against flights. Little Black, Blossom, Elsinore and Torrey are calibrated this way; the other sites are educated guesses. It never replaces your own check of the real forecast and conditions on launch.

How a score is made

1. The flying window. Three hours, placed each day where the lift is best (height first, thermal strength second, never the wind), somewhere between 10 am and the start of glass-off.

2. Height and strength in that window. Height is the top of usable lift, as the usual soaring forecasts show it, read at the launch and at points over the terrain around it (see the charts below): the four best readings, at most two from one point. It is scored on each site's own scale, e.g. Little Black 1,800 ft a good local day, 2,300 ft XC, 3,500 ft great. Strength is shown as the climb rate in the core: 1.4 × W* minus a paraglider's sink of 1.1 m/s.

3. Deductions, the same everywhere:

4. XC and Hammerday. XC needs the site's XC height, workable winds and a climb rate of at least 0.5 m/s. A 5 needs the site's great height, wind into launch and no deductions at all; otherwise 4 is the top.

5. Glass-off. The last 3 hours before sunset are rated on their own (3 at most). If that beats the day, the site shows "Glass-off".

Sites with their own rules

Torrey is a ridge: wind decides. A forecast 8 mph from WSW–WNW is soarable (the real wind is usually a bit stronger), 6–8 mph is marginal, under 6 mph usually not flyable. Cloud and a chance of showers don't count, a likely-wet day does (45% of runs −1, 60% −1.5). Over 20 mph or gusts over 25 is too windy; blown out a quarter of the day caps it at 3, half the day at 1.5. Straight south or north is a no-go. A 4 needs a steady WSW–WNW in the afternoon; a thermal day there takes the full deductions.

Elsinore needs an east launch wind or calm, then the west switch; the card says when the switch comes (or "no switch"). It keeps an 11 am–4 pm window.

Laguna / Horse / Fuzz is a convergence site: each launch is scored on its own and it needs the convergence to rate above 2.

Trip areas (experimental): Santa Barbara / Ojai and Owens Valley are rated as areas: each main launch on its own, the best one counts, and height is read over the launches and the terrain between them. Owens is only for big, calm days: wind at the top of lift over 10 mph or over 8 mph on launch makes it 2 at most, over 15 mph at the top is a no-go, and outside its season it shows 2 at most.

Models

HRRR (counted twice) and RRFS while HRRR covers the day, then RRFS (twice) with GFS, ECMWF and ICON, then the global models alone. On the RRFS days the wind comes from RRFS and ECMWF only: GFS and ICON count for lift, not for wind. The ensembles give the chance of flying and of XC. The NWS San Diego discussion adds showers and the day summary. The main page lists every source with its run time.

What it doesn't know

Real-time observations, local wind effects beyond the rules above, turbulence and rotor, overdevelopment, site closures, permits and airspace. The day type and the direction of the wind aloft aren't scored (except a small nick at Blossom for drift toward the Rock Pile). Five to seven days out it runs on global models only, so expect those days to move.

Where the radar measures

The wind always comes from the launch itself. For the height of lift, most sites also look at points around the launch, because the best climbs are often over the hills behind it, not right above take-off.
