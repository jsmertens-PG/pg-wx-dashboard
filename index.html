How the rating works

0–10 scale. A nice local thermal or glass-off day rates about 3.5–5 (green). XC days start at 6: about 6 is a short or low XC, 7–8 a solid one, 9 a big day. The rating blends the median of five models (HRRR, NAM, GFS, ECMWF, ICON) with the average of about 120 ensemble runs.

Each hour, 9 am to 6 pm, is checked for rain and storms, wind direction and strength on launch, wind aloft (both strength and direction, because north or east wind aloft kills most of these sites), and how high a dry thermal from the surface gets before it meets the temperature profile or cloudbase.

Altitude and wind matter most. Only real rain or storms are a no-go; scattered light showers cost a little. Elsinore and Laguna/Horse are rated as convergence sites, so strong wind aloft from any direction counts against them. Torrey is rated on ridge wind, but a day with thermals well above the cliffs is flagged as a Thermal day.

Labels: XC, Thermal day (Torrey), Thermal (local), Glass-off (smooth onshore flow from 4 pm on), Ridge soaring (Torrey), Marginal, No go. Tags add post-frontal (rain earlier or cold air aloft) and later glass-off. XC notes suggest a direction from the drift and each site's routes, and warn when the drift heads into airspace or the border.

Limits for each site are in sites.json. They are starting values to tune against real days.
